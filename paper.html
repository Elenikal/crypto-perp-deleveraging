Leverage Before the Crash

When a crypto market falls hard, how much of it is the margin engine rather than the news? Open interest destroyed during the fall is the trace forced selling leaves behind.

Method. Hourly panel of five Binance USD-M perpetuals. An episode is the worst 2% of 6-hour drawdowns in each market, thinned to one per 24 hours. Event time zero is the end of the drawdown window, so no trough is chosen with hindsight. Leverage is open interest over its own trailing 30-day mean, measured before the episode starts. Standard errors are clustered by calendar day, because these five markets fall together and 437 episodes come from only 170 days.

Two measurement results worth knowing before you try this. At 5-minute frequency the open-interest proxy is noise: it moves with returns at a correlation near zero, and in the worst 5-minute bars open interest is as likely to rise as fall. At hourly frequency the same proxy correlates strongly with returns. Separately, open interest divided by volume, the obvious leverage measure, correlates -0.87 with volume itself. It measures how quiet a market is, not how levered.

Limits. Forced flow is proxied, not observed: true liquidation records are behind a paid feed. Five markets, one venue, two years. Finding 2 does not survive splitting the sample, and is reported as fragile rather than dropped.

Reproduce. python3 fetch.py && python3 analyze.py, about 240 MB of free public files, no account and no credentials.
